# Model Filtering

We have various different types of model relationships:

- Some relationships are fields directly on the model (`variant_of`, `remake_of`, `export_edition_of`)
- Others are an edge (the `ModelRelationship` join table)
- Some relationships, like bootleg, are a edge with licensing status: bootleg = (copy, unlicensed) on `ModelRelationship`.
- Export market is a relationship where the target isn't a Model but a Location. That's the `ModelExportMarket` join table. The Location must have a null parent: i.e., a Country.
- It'd also be nice to filter models by their Corporate Entity's locations. That's `MachineModel` → `CorporateEntity` → `CorporateEntityLocation` → `Location`. These locations are NOT null parents: they are usually cities, which have parent states/regions, which have parent countries. The hierarchy depth is NOT fixed, it depends on the country. Different countries organize their geographics into different units. But I sense this one is stretching the ask too much

I've been struggling with how to make all these relationships filterable in places like the titles listing page, the API, re-usable by Articles.md. I don't have a good theory of mind of how all of these things consolidate into one easily addressable thing.

I want to be able to explore both sides of a relationship. For example, for bootleg (copy, unlicensed): I want to be able to:

- filter to ONLY bootleg models
- filter OUT bootleg models
- filter to ONLY models that have had bootlegs made of it

I'd like to be able to combine these with other data, such as:

- show the models that have been copied the most
- show the manufacturers that have been copied the most
  ... but I'm not sure yet where I'd show this information.

As-is, the Export API contains these relationships and consumers could technically filter client-side, but it'd be a pretty big hassle: the consumer must first spend quite a lot of time understanding the relationships, THEN design the filtering. It'd be more friendly to offer a filtering API that does the job for them. It would return model and title slugs (and IDs?), and consumers would get the details of each record from their cached copy of the Export API results.

Apparently we have a `GET /api/models/` that isn't being used internally; I'd be okay with repurposing that as the filter API.

This is NOT for v1; it'd be a follow-up.

## Examples

They're grouped by the _shape_ of the question rather than by subject, because the shapes are what the architecture has to answer to — and the groups get progressively more demanding.

### One relationship, one side

The simplest shape: models carrying a given relationship.

- **Conversion kits** — every kit that turns one model into another (Geiger, Good Year, Sky Warrior).
- **Conversions** — every model actually built out of a donor model (the j-martina games).
- **Rethemes** — every model that reskins another one's gameplay with new art (Metallica reskins, Shrek).
- **Copies** — every model reproducing another's design on new hardware.
- **Bootlegs** — unlicensed copies.
- **Licensed copies** — the opposite: models built _with_ permission, like VIFICO's run under license from Gottlieb.
- **Remakes** — every remake (Medieval Madness is the banner one).
- **Export editions** — models built as the export version of a domestic original.

### Excluding a relationship

- **Filter OUT bootlegs.**
- **Filter out conversion kits** — The Flip's manufacturer timeline counts commercially produced models and doesn't consider a kit to be one.

### The other side of a relationship

- **Models that have had bootlegs made of them.**
- **Models that have been copied at all** — the original, not the copy.
- **All the variants of one game** — every variant of Godzilla, on a Godzilla-related article.
- **Models that have been remade.**

### Composed with location

- **Italian bootlegs** — every unlicensed copy made in Italy (the RMG models and their kind).
- **Bootlegs everywhere** — every unlicensed copy, full stop: Spain (Petaco, Maresa), Italy (RMG), Brazil (LTD do Brasil), all in one place.
- **The Chicago bingo-pinball industry and the gambling laws** — bingo-pinball models or titles from Chicago firms. Notably this needs no relationship at all: it's game format crossed with location.
- **Spanish EM manufacturers** — the story of the Spanish electromechanical era.
- **Models copied across a national boundary** — Spanish firms copying American designs.

### Composed with other catalog fields

- **One manufacturer's whole output** — every model or title that RMG ever built.
- **All the variants for a specific manufacturer** — rather than for a specific model.
- **The rise of the remake industry** — remakes over time; the story is the clustering, not the list.
- **How export models were adapted to comply with different countries' laws** — exports to Italy or Spain, and how those models differ by reward type.

### Constraining the far end of the relationship

Not just "does this relationship exist" but "what's on the other end of it".

- **All the Williams models or titles that have been copied by other firms** — inbound copies, excluding Williams copying itself.
- **Models or titles copied by Spanish firms** — the far end is a manufacturer in a given country.
- **Conversions built from Gottlieb donors.**
- **Conversion kits that fit an EM model or title.**

### Multiple relationships at once

- **Models that are both a copy and a conversion** — a bootlegger who reproduces someone's design _and_ builds it on converted donor cabinets holds two true facts at once.
- **Models that were both remade and copied.**

### Rankings and superlatives

- **The most copied models of all time.**
- **The most copied titles of all time.**
- **The manufacturers that have been copied the most.**
- **The first variant ever created.**
- **The widest gap between an original and its copy.**

### Statistics across a group

These don't return a list of models at all; the answer is a number per group, or a pairing.

- **The average variant count per model, for each modern manufacturer.**
- **Manufacturer pairings** — how much one manufacturer copied (or exported to) another. Maresa and Petaco copying Gottlieb, Bally Wulff copying Bally, Segasa copying Williams. The interesting rendering of this is probably a matrix or a chord diagram rather than a list.

### Curator worklists

Not reader-facing articles — these surface where the catalog is thin, which is how the data gets better.

- **Models whose donor is unknown** — the relationships recorded with a free-text label instead of a resolved model. What we know that we don't know.
- **Export editions with no export market recorded** — a model flagged as an export edition where nobody has said where it was exported to.
- **Copies with no licensing status** — where the licensing question has never been researched either way.

### A note on "models or titles"

Many examples above say "models or titles". That means the per-scenario grain thinking hasn't been done. It's not a requirement that every query work both ways.

### Don't treat examples as MUST do

Don't treat each of the above examples as MUST do. I don't know the cost of each one. They may stretch the architecture in ways it doesn't make sense. You _must_ push back if there's a better, simpler, more composable version of the architecture that maybe doesn't support every single little feature implied by the examples, but composes better with future features.

#### Where each example landed

Every example above has a row in the [coverage ledger](ModelFilteringPlan.md#coverage-ledger) — in v1, after v1, rejected, or belonging on some other surface. Cutting one means changing its row, not quietly omitting it.

## <a id="games-page"></a>`/games` listing page

We explored how to do filtering in [filtering UX research](#filtering-ux-research) and came to a few conclusions:

- We want to continue being Title-first, but when necessary we also need to finer-grained results, heterogenously: Title, Non-Variant Parent Models, Variant Models. Because sometimes a model or a variant model is the only thing that matches the search/filter. This applies both to global search and when using filters.
- We should replace `/titles` with a `/games` listing page that finds these heterogenous results.
- These results should respect [Title > Model > Variant roll-up](#rollup) rules.

## <a id="rollup"></a>Title > Model > Variant roll-up

More precisely, these are the `Title` ➡️ `Non-Variant Model` ➡️ `Variant Model` rollup rules.

Why these groups? They're the most semantically important:

- A Title groups models that the pinball community things of as a single title.
- A Variant is gameplay-identical to a parent Model. Even though each Variant has a separate SKU from its parent machine, tournaments group Variants with their parent Model.

The basic idea is to show the highest-level grouping that satisfies the query.

For example, searching for...

- `q=Godzilla` returns only the Godzilla titles and no models because all Godzilla models roll up to them.
- `q=Godzilla Premium` returns only the `godzilla-premium` non-variant model (not the title or variants), because that's the highest-level item that matches.
- `q=Godzilla Anniversary` returns only the `godzilla-70th-anniversary` variant model, because that's the only one that matches.

Note that these examples don't actually work because of punctuation; the name is Godzilla (Premium). Punctuation-aware search is part of the plan but not for v1.

### The rules

- To show a Title, the Title must satisfy every Title-level dimension AND every one of its active child Models (including Variants) must satisfy every Model-only dimension.
- To show any Model (including Variants), the Model must satisfy every Model-level dimension and its Title must not be showing, and its Title must satisfy every Title-only dimension.
- To show a Variant Model, additionally its parent Model must not be showing.

#### Dimensions are tested on the record that owns it

The dimensions are spread across Model and Title:

- Title and Model share: name, abbreviations.
- Title-only dimensions: franchise, series.
- Model-only dimensions: manufacturer, year, tech generation, themes, gameplay features, edges etc.

Each dimension is tested on the record that owns it:

- Title-only dimensions (franchise, series) are tested on the Title, and bind its Models.
- Model-only dimensions (manufacturer, year, tech generation...) are tested on the Model, and a Title passes only when every one of its Models does.
- Dimensions that occur on both (name, abbreviations) are tested on the record being decided, and never propagate up or down.

#### Description is not a dimension

The description field -- only searchable from Global Search -- is a tier, not a dimension. Decription can only add records, never absorb or replace one. If a title's description contains "Model X", the results ADD the Title, and it does NOT replace the "Model X" model result.

### Multi-select

When selecting multiple items in a multi-select -- Themes, Gameplay Features -- each result should have ALL of them.

- A Model card appears only if that Model carries every selected theme.
- A Title card appears only if every Model in it carries every selected theme.

This is a change from the current code, which looks across a Title for any model that matches a portion of the AND.

### Examples

- `q=Ice Fever` should show Title "Ice Fever" even though the Title also contains a model called "Ice Mania".
- `q=Ice Mania` should show Model "Ice Mania" -- Title's "Ice Fever" name fails.
- `franchise=harley-davidson&manufacturer=sega` should show the Harley models from Sega but not the ones from Stern or other manufacturers.
- `q=Godzilla Premium` shows only `godzilla-premium`. Does not match the Title, because those are named "Godzilla".
- `franchise=star-wars` all Star Wars Titles
- `theme=monster`. If Godzilla Premium doesn't have theme=monster but its variant Godzilla 70th Anniversary does, do not show premium but do show anniversay. If premium DOES have theme=monster, roll up and don't show anniversary.
- `manufacturer=vifico`. The manufacturer VIFICO only made copies of Gottlieb models. Those copies are under the same Title as the copied Gottlieb model. Filtering by `manufacturer=vifico` should not show the Title because not all models are VIFICO, so instead it shows the individual VIFICO Models.
- `remake`. The manufacturer Chicago Gaming remade Medieval Madness. The remakes share a Title with the original. Filtering by `remake` should show the individual Chicago Gaming remake models becuase the Title has the non-`remake` original.
- `manufacturer=chicago-gaming` should show each individual Medieval Madness remake Model, not the Title, because the title has the non-Chicago-Gaming original.
- `q=Karate Fight` should show the "Black Belt / Karate Fight" Title. That Title holds two Models: Black Belt (Bally 1986) and Karate Fight (Bally Wulff 1986). Since the query matches Title's own name, the Title card is shown and not its Models.
- `q=Ice Fever&theme=X` should return nothing if the Ice Fever model doesn't have theme X, even if its sister model Ice Mania in the same Title does have theme X.
- Global search on `Model X`: if a title's description contains "Model X", the results ADD the Title, and it does NOT replace the "Model X" model result.

### Sorting

Continue sorting by newest year first.

### Stop matching on representative model

Currently we sometimes match a Title's representative Model. `/titles` currently filters by "ANY of its active non-variant models satisfies X" for 11 of 13 dimensions; however, `manufacturer` and `q` are pinned to the representative.

Here's what a representative model is. A Title has a name and maybe a franchise or series attached to it, but the rest of the data lies on its Models: backglass images, manufacturer, year, tech generation, gameplay features, themes, etc. The Title itself does not contain enough information to show a compelling result card; people want to see a backglass image, manufacturer and year. So when we display a Title card, we pick a representative Model from which to source that information. To choose the representative Model, we first exclude all Models that are marked as variants. Variants are gameplay-identical to a parent model, and never head their Title. We then downrank (we call it 'subordinate') other Models that we consider secondary -- copies, export models etc -- so that more primary, non-subordinated Models float to the top. And then we choose the earliest year model. Tiebreak is alphabetical name, but that's arbitrary.

Trying to reason about filtering by representative model got super complicated. It would have been an endless source of edge cases that nobody would ever be able to remember or reason about. By no longer filtering on representative Model, it returns to it's original display-only purpose. It answers "which backglass and year does this Title card show", nothing else.

### What roll-up gives up

As I recall, the idea behind how the filtering currently works was to show Title when filtering by things visible on the Title card (manufacturer and year). This would let casual users filter by year and still just see the one Godzilla Stern 2021 title rather than the four sub-models. That was probably a wrong call: once you're filtering, you've self-identified as a bit of a geek interested in accurate data and probably want to see Models.

Only ~450 titles (~7% of the catalog) have more than one model, so roll-up doesn't affect _that_ much, but it's the most modern looked-at portion, so it's importat.

#### Sparse data shatters

A Title rolls up only when **every** live Model matches, so one single unrecorded value on one single Model is enough to break it — the Model does not match, and the Title stops being unanimous.

This is okay. We're going to treat this as a feature not a bug. Models are missing information should be updated. Today the gap is invisible and hence is harder to address. The shattering makes it more visible.

### Facet counts

The `/titles` page displays a count badge next to each filter, giving a very useful sense of how many results clicking the filter will reduce to, and I want to carry over counts to `/games`. That count currently counts Titles. However, in a heterogenous listing that's probably not appropriate.

The easiest thing to do would be to make these Model counts, always. Even when those Models roll up to Titles. I don't love this; I'd love for it to roll up. This is what users expect: count = visible items. But that's probably very expensive.

## Dimension detail pages

Detail pages like `/themes/medieval` and `/franchise/godzilla` that are the detail page for a dimension on Model or Title should roll up, same as `/games?theme=medieval` and `/games?franchise=godzilla`. No new filtering logic.

This is NOT a change for Title dimension detail pages like `/franchise/godzilla`; they already show Titles.

This IS a change for Model dimension detail pages. Most, like `/themes/medieval`, currently list Models. Manufacturers and corporate entities, currently list Titles.

Note that theme detail pages like `/themes/sports` currently only show games tagged directly with that theme, rather than rolling up child themes like `horse-racing`. This is an oversight; using the same logic as `/games?theme=sports` will fix it.

### Dimension detail pages should infinite scroll

Some dimension detail pages do not paginate. This is an oversight; almost every list on the site has infinite scrolling and these should be no different.

### Hidden dimensions

The current `/titles` listing page doesn't contain some dimensions, like display subtypes etc. To consolidate all the taxonomy detail pages on to a single API shape, the API must include those dimensions, but let's continue to hide those dimensions from the /games UI. The `/games` query string can include and honor them (or not, if that's easier), but I don't want their controls showing in the list of filters because they just aren't very useful. If it's easier, show the controls once the page is being filtered by it.

## Sparse dimension fields

Some values for Model fields are sparsely populated:

- `game_format = pinball`
- `production_status = produced`
- `cabinet = floor`

For example, only a small number of Models are explicitly classified as `game_format=pinball` even though most are ordinary pinball machines. Most Models have null for these fields, meaning “not classified.”

Problems:

- `/games` page: a Game Format filter with a `Pinball` option will only show a smattering of already-classified Models
- `/game_format/pinball` page: already only shows a smattering of already-classified Models

To fix this, we want these pages to treat `null` as `pinball` (or `produced` or `floor`). I want this to be a shallow UI fix: lump them together in the filter, such that both `pinball` + `null` are sent back on the API. This way we can also use the API to find true nulls.

This must NOT change how these values are displayed anywhere in the product. The edit pages must continue to treat them as nulls. The read-only detail view must continue to not show anything at all for the default `pinball` / `null` case.

## Filtering UX research

Filtering (which currently lives on the `/titles` listing page) and global search currently only display Title cards. However, these are relationships between **models**. Most of the filters are filtering information on **models**: manufacturer, player count, technology generation, etc.

There's a tension here: the site is Title-first, because most of the public that visits the site are not hard-core enthusiasts and don't know or care that a title has different models. However, the average non-enthusiast is NOT using filters: I'll argue that identifies you as an enthusiast and I'll further wager you are more likely than not looking for specific models.

### What we're not doing

The naive answer would be to add a `/models` listing page that sits in the site's top nav alongside `/titles`, but our stance is that most of the public that visits the site are not hard-core enthusiasts and don't know or care that a title has different models. Comparable sites for other catories have made the same decision, such as:

- Goodreads: Work vs Edition. Shows the most popular edition, with a quiet "1,247 editions" link. Casual readers never notice editions exist.
- BoardGameGeek: a game's "versions" (printings, language editions) are a sub-tab on the game page. Never browsable, never in nav.

Having top-level navigation to both Titles and Models commits a cardinal sin: it makes the user think. It creates a complex choice right up front for casual users of the site, forcing them to reason about our data model.

### Data points

- 93.4% of titles are single-model, where the UI already collapses Title and Model into one page.

### Options

#### ❌ Separate search surfaces, entry points not in top nav

We have a `/models` listing page but it's reached from prose and links, not the top bar. Prior art:

- Wikipedia — "List of X" articles are model-grain lists you arrive at from an article, not from nav.
- TV Tropes — concept pages that are curated cross-linked lists of works.

Rejected: I want a reliable way of getting to the model filtering. I don't want to have to go ferret around for a list that happens to link to it.

#### ❌ One search surface, with a toggle

A single `/machines` or `/games` listing page, with a toggle for [title|model] that defaults to title.

Rejected: a [title|model] toggle would do nothing at all for 93% of what it shows; it changes about one row in ten. That's a lot of "make the user think" for a small delta — and it's the same objection to a nav split, just relocated.

#### One search surface, grain as a facet

A single listing page. Browse returns a heterogeneous set; a Type facet in the sidebar narrows it. Prior art:

- Discogs search has a Type facet (Release / Master / Artist / Label).
- WorldCat / FRBR-ized library catalogs — search returns works, with format and edition as facets. "View all editions and formats" is the expander.

The reason this doesn't violate your cardinal sin: the control lives in the filter sidebar with the other facets, not in the top nav. People already filtering find it. Everyone else never sees it.

#### One search surface, switch by result-shape not entity

A single listing page. Under the search bar, or in the filter pane, there's a "[ ] Group models by title" control changes whether to show Titles or Models. Prior art:

Amazon, Google Shopping — "group variants" behavior on the results grid.

The framing difference matters more than it sounds. "Titles vs Models" asks the user to learn your data model. "Group models by title: ✓" asks them to understand a display preference. Same query, no ontology lesson.

#### One search surface, filter determines grain

Single listing page. Have the "[ ] Group models by title" control, checked by default. When you select a filter that only makes sense for models ("filter to bootlegs"), it:

- Unchecks the "[ ] Group models by title" control
- Potentially also displays "showing individual models because you filtered by Bootleg"

Pros:

- Demands nothing of casual users.

Cons:

- We're having a hard time thinking of existing systems that do this. We prefer well-established patterns. It's a smell, like maybe we haven't figured out the real cons.

Open questions:

- Keep with `/titles` or change to `/machines` or `/games`?
- We need a written, deterministic rule about which filters flip to models. I'll argue that almost every filter is model-specific: manufacturer, player count, technology generation, etc etc etc. I'll further argue that if you're geeking out on filters like this, you are more likely than not looking for specific models. Though maybe we roll up variants somehow, to clean up the list?

#### ❌ Only title search, model-aware title card

Continue only having the `/titles` listing page, but make the Title card context-aware of what Model was actually found.

For example, "Big Valley (R.M.G.)" is an unlicensed copy of "Big Valley (Bally)". On the `/titles` page if you filter by 'bootleg' and search for 'Big Valley', you'd get the Big Valley Title card and it'd still link to `/title/big-valley`, but it'd show info about the actual model found, something like:

```text
Big Valley — R.M.G. 1970
Unlicensed copy of Big Valley (Bally 1970)
2 machines
```

Maybe when you click through to the title page we carry information on the query string about which of the models to highlight: `/title/big-valley?model=big-valley-rmg`.

Pros:

- Demands nothing of casual users.

Cons:

Both of these are showstoppers:

- **Doesn't link to the model found**. If your goal is to find "Big Valley (R.M.G.)", you click to the Title page, then have to re-find "Big Valley (R.M.G.)" on that page.
- **Doesn't show models**. If your goal is to get a list of all bootleg Models, you don't. You get a list of all Titles containing bootlegs.

Systems that do this:

- Amazon swaps the product image to the variant you filtered by. Clicking through takes you to the product page, but tuned to the variant. We, however, probably do not want to 'tune' our title page to highlight a specific model (`/title/big-valley?model=big-valley-rmg`). Users probably want to go to the specific model page.
- Google search results show the matching passage, not the first paragraph (this is a loose analogy to our title card always showing the 'primary' title)

### Prior art

Look at music sites whose search results include tracks. That's very similar to the heterogenous hierarchical grain results we have: track, album, artist.

#### Discogs

I'm looking at Discogs now for the first time:

- When I search for a track, it shows me the albums that the track is in, but I have to take it on faith that that's what's happening, there's no indication. That's a bit like searching for "Godzilla Pro" and getting the Godzilla Title instead of the actual model. Not sure I like it. I was expecting it to show me the track.
- On that same search, it shows me multiple versions of the same album: Master Release (the first item), Vinyl, Viynl Ltd Edition 2024 Reiussue, Vinyl Ltd Edition 2023 Reissue, and 10 more. So it's NOT rolling up, it's showing this fake Master Release thing along with it.
- I didn't understand what a Master Release was, even after you had warned me I'd be seeing it. I had to google what it was, which most casual users won't do. This is a lesson I didn't need to re-learn: don't invent your own metaphors and force them on people.
- I see what Discogs did with Master Release: they didn't have a grouping concept, so they shoehorned one in by creating a fake album type called Master Release, which they treat special. We're definitely not taking this approach.
- They put the grain switcher up at the top, as tabs: All (the default view), Release, Master, Artist, Label. Release filters out Master Release, Master filters out everything but Master Release. I was looking all over their filter sidebar for the option. I hate it when apps spread the filtering controls all over in different modalities.

However, that works best when the Album and the Track are named separate things. Searching for the eponymous title track "Iron Maiden", in the eponymous album "Iron Maiden" of the band Iron Maiden: all I get is the band. Not the album or the title.

Roll-up, backend engine, frontend card unification.

Don't leave the system in some messy halfway migrated state; rename and consolidate. For example:

- No parallel paths. There must be exactly one way to answer
  "which records match these filters", one listing card schema and one
  listing card component.
- No shims. No aliases, no re-exports, no deprecation comments, no flags
  added to preserve the old behavior.
- When a symbol is renamed its file, tests, fixtures
  and docstrings move with it. Grep the old symbol before merging.
- Delete the things that you replace.
- Specific examples
  - Update labels like "6,180 titles" -> "6,180 games"
  - Rename card schemas (don't keep `TitleCardSchema`, it'd be a lie)
  - Rename `/api/titles/` -> `/api/games/`. Do not keep `/api/titles/`.

At the same time I'm afraid AI sessions will spam the word 'Game' everywhere. The word 'game' is an invitation to fuzzy thinking; usually we mean exactly Title or exactly Model, not either/both. Only use "game" where you must, to indicate either/both.

Do NOT rename Svelte route; that's a separate PR.

It must prove it out on Postgres. Use [Docker Postgres](../../Data.md#postgres). It must ALSO prove that it works on SQLite because that's the dev DB. SQLite perf is secondary.

###### Outcome

The proving commit landed and the foundation holds: the roll-up is implementable, card counts are affordable (so the facet badges count visible cards, not Models) and the engine is small enough to hand to the next maintainer. It is slower than the Title-only system by design, but the cost lands where the new work runs: the unfiltered listing is barely slower (+38% at the query layer, since the roll-up aggregates only run when a Model-only filter is active), filtered requests and the facet fan-out run roughly 1.4–2×, and the cached no-filter facet path is unaffected; the user-visible delta is smaller still because hydration and serialization are unchanged. One implementation decision, the row seam (SQL union vs Python merge), is provisional until re-measured on Railway hardware, because the benchmark ran on a fast laptop with a local database and that is the one decision that trades database work against app work. Figures, decisions and the re-measurement instructions: [the plan's answers](./ModelFilteringPlan.md#prove-answers).
